How the hierarchy gets built on day one

Your design — only National seeded, each level creating the one below it, and officer fields as dropdowns with no encoding — is correct, and it exposes a circular dependency my last version hid rather than solved. Here is the deadlock, the one rule that breaks it, and the exact order of operations for go-live.

1 · The deadlock, stated exactly

Take it one step at a time and it closes on itself.

To do this
Seat a city council officer
Dropdown only — he must be an existing member
You need
A registered member
Members are created by chapters only
You need
A registered chapter
Approved by its city council
You need
A seated city council
Which is where you started

The same ring closes at every level below National, and it is why my previous answer invented provisional seats — accounts that could approve without being members. You were right to reject that. It solved the deadlock by putting people in the hierarchy who were not in the register, which is exactly the thing the register exists to prevent.

2 · The rule that breaks it

You already approved this mechanism, for a different problem.

Approval routing
An application is approved by the nearest existing ancestor.
If the city council does not exist, the province approves. If the province does not exist, the region does. If the region does not exist, National does.

This is the same escalation you already accepted for renewals, where a province may act when a city council does not. It turns out that one rule solves three separate problems, which is usually a sign it is the right rule:

SituationWho approves
Normal operationThe immediate parent — city council approves its chapters
Bootstrap — no council exists yet in that jurisdictionThe nearest ancestor that does exist
Dormancy — the council exists but has no seated officersThe nearest ancestor with seated officers
Delay — the council exists and will not actThe ancestor, on override, after the window lapses
Nothing new is being built here. The escalation ladder, the override record and the working-day clock all already exist in the design. Bootstrap becomes a special case of a mechanism you have already agreed to, rather than a separate concept.

3 · The second rule, and it inverts something I told you earlier

Council creation
A council can only be created once at least one registered chapter exists in its jurisdiction.
Chapters come first. The council is constituted over them.

I told you the opposite last time — that a council is created empty and chapters attach afterwards. That was wrong, and your dropdown rule is what makes it wrong. If the officer fields can only be dropdowns, then the dropdown must have something in it, and the only source of members is chapters in that jurisdiction.

It also matches how the organization actually works. A city council with no chapters governs nothing. Requiring a chapter first is not a technical constraint dressed up as policy — it is the policy, and the technical constraint happens to agree with it.

4 · The order of operations at go-live

One seeded thing. Everything after it is the normal process.

0

Seed the National Council

SYSTEM ADMIN · ONCE · AT INSTALLATION

Two rows and nothing else: the National Council body, and a System Admin technical account. The System Admin is not a council officer and holds no office — he is the installer, and his approval authority is temporary.

What does not happen here
No member is created. No officer is seated. No chapter exists.
1

The first chapters register

PILOT CHAPTERS → APPROVED BY NATIONAL

Your pilot chapters file the chapter registration form — the one with the eight officers on it. There is no city council, no province and no region yet, so by the routing rule the National Council is the nearest existing ancestor and approves them directly.

This is the moment the register comes into existence. Every member in the system traces back to one of these chapter registrations.

Members now existChapters now exist
2

The National Council seats its own officers

SYSTEM ADMIN → DROPDOWN FROM STEP 1 MEMBERS

Now the dropdown has people in it. The National Council's president, vice president, secretary, treasurer, auditor and PIO are selected from the members created in step 1. No encoding.

The System Admin steps back here. Once the National Council has seated officers, the installer's approval authority ends. He keeps technical access — backups, restores, the audit log — and loses the ability to approve anything organizational. That handover should be an explicit, logged, one-way event.
3

National creates the first region

NATIONAL COUNCIL → OFFICERS FROM CHAPTERS IN THAT REGION

Region IV-A is created. Its officers are selected from members of the pilot chapters that sit inside Region IV-A — which is why step 1 pilots should be chosen in the region you intend to stand up first.

Practical consequence for your pilot choice
Pick pilot chapters that are concentrated in one city, in one province, in one region. A pilot scattered across four regions cannot seat any council except National.
4

Region creates the province, province creates the city council

EACH LEVEL → OFFICERS FROM CHAPTERS IN ITS JURISDICTION

Exactly as you described. Region IV-A creates Laguna Provincial Council and seats its officers from members of Laguna chapters. Laguna creates Sta. Rosa City Council and seats its officers from members of Sta. Rosa chapters.

Each of these is the same screen with the same dropdown. One form, reused four times.

5

Steady state

NORMAL OPERATION FROM HERE ON

New chapters in Sta. Rosa now go to Sta. Rosa City Council, because it exists. The routing rule stops escalating because the immediate parent is there. Nothing about the process changes — the same rule simply resolves to a different council.

Chapters → city council Renewals → city council Ancestors only on dormancy or delay

5 · Expanding into a new province later

The same sequence runs again, and it is worth seeing that it needs no special handling:

  • Chapters in an unserved city register. Their city council does not exist, so the province approves them — nearest existing ancestor.
  • Once there are chapters there, the province creates the city council and seats its officers from those chapters' members.
  • From then on those chapters route to their own city council.

Bootstrap is not a one-time event that happens at installation. It is what happens at the edge of the organization every time it grows, so it has to be an ordinary path rather than a migration script.

6 · What this removes from the design

ConceptStatus
Provisional seats Delete entirely No longer needed. The routing rule covers every case they were invented for, without putting non-members in the hierarchy.
Council-created members Already removed Members are created by chapters only. Unchanged.
"A council is created empty" Reversed A council requires at least one chapter in its jurisdiction before it can be created.
Detached members Keep Still real, and unrelated to bootstrap: a member whose chapter went dormant after he was already registered.
Escalation ladder and override Keep, and reuse Bootstrap becomes a case of it rather than a separate mechanism.
The design gets smaller, not bigger. One table and one concept come out, and a rule you had already accepted does more work. That is usually the sign of a correct simplification rather than a clever one.

7 · Three things I need you to decide

1 · Must a council officer belong to a chapter inside that council's jurisdiction? My recommendation is yes — a Laguna provincial officer should be a member of a Laguna chapter. It keeps the dropdown meaningful and matches how councils are constituted. But it means that if a province has only one chapter, that chapter supplies all six council officers, which may be unworkable in practice. Do you want a waiver, and if so who grants it?
2 · How long may an ancestor keep approving in place of a missing council? Indefinitely is the simple answer and it is probably right — a jurisdiction with no chapters does not need a council. But it means a province could quietly hold twenty chapters that ought to have had a city council years ago. Do you want the national dashboard to flag a jurisdiction with, say, five or more chapters and no seated council?
3 · What exactly does the System Admin lose at step 2, and can it be restored? My recommendation is that his organizational approval authority ends permanently and cannot be self-restored — restoring it should require the National Council. The alternative is a permanent technical account that can approve anything, which is the single most dangerous object in the system. But you need a defined answer for the case where the National Council's seated officers all become unreachable at once.
